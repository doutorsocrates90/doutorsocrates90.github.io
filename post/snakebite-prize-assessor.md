The Wellcome Snakebite Innovation Prize is a £6.25 million challenge prize that backs technological, social, logistical and systems-level innovations to strengthen community responses to snakebite, accelerate access to appropriate care and improve the delivery of treatment, in support of the World Health Organization's goal of halving snakebite deaths and disability by 2030.

As a member of the pool of independent **Technical Assessors**, I carried out the first-line review of entries across the prize's two tracks, **Launch** (early-stage concepts) and **Growth** (solutions already in validation or scale-up). Each entry was scored against the published judging criteria and written up in a structured assessor judgment for the Judging Panel:

- **Impact** and the credibility of the pathway to patient outcomes
- **Innovation** and contribution to the field
- **Implementation pathway and sustainability**
- **Understanding of the end user**, including communities, front-line workers and health systems
- **Team capability and project delivery**, including budget realism
- **Safety and ethics**, as a pass/fail gate

Assessing this portfolio meant reading across very different solution types (digital tools, diagnostics, community networks, logistics, training) and judging them with the same evidence-first lens I use as a research and innovation consultant: what is asserted, what is demonstrated, and what would it take for this to work where snakebite is most harmful.
